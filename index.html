COMMON OBSTACLES

How We Help You Navigate Credit Challenges

Understanding and challenging negative items can be difficult. We help you address the practical issues that affect everyday credit reports.

Inaccurate Late-Payment Records

Payments marked 30, 60, or 90 days delinquent due to billing cycle confusion, bank transitions, or clerical data entry errors.

Incorrect Collection Accounts

Collection agencies reporting unverified debts, duplicate entries from sold accounts, or balances belonging to another individual with a similar name.

Balance & Limit Discrepancies

Credit card balances reported higher than actual balances, or credit limits missing entirely, which unfairly damages credit utilization ratios.

Outdated Negative Information

Negative accounts that continue to appear on your report past the federal 7-year reporting window (or 10 years for certain bankruptcies) contrary to the FCRA.

Complex Tri-Bureau Reports

Deciphering dense bureau codes, differing account numbers, and contradictory entries between Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion without guidance.

Dispute Uncertainty & Next Steps

Confusion over how to submit supporting documentation, what timeline credit bureaus have to respond, and what to do if an error is verified incorrectly.

Important Notice Regarding Credit Repair Compliance

Every credit file is unique, and items are audited strictly on a case-by-case basis. Under the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), no credit repair company can legally promise or guarantee score increases, deletion of accurate and verifiable accounts, or specific outcomes. Veritas Credit focuses on helping consumers identify genuine inaccuracies and enforce their statutory rights under federal law.

TRANSPARENT ANSWERS

Frequently Asked Questions

Get clear, honest information about credit repair, federal consumer protection, and what to expect.

Credit repair is the lawful process of reviewing your credit reports across the three major bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion), identifying inaccurate, obsolete, incomplete, or unverifiable information, and exercising your rights under federal law to request investigations and corrections.

We begin with a line-by-line review of your credit reports. When questionable or inaccurate entries are identified, we help compile supporting documentation and prepare formal dispute packages sent directly to the reporting bureaus or original furnishers under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). The recipients have a statutory window (usually 30 days) to investigate and either verify, correct, or delete the disputed item.

By law, credit bureaus have 30 to 45 days to investigate dispute claims once received. However, because credit profiles often require multiple rounds of review, documentation gathering, and creditor verification, a thorough process typically spans 3 to 6 months depending on the number and complexity of items under review.

No. Under the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), no ethical credit repair provider can promise or guarantee the removal of accurate, timely, and verifiable negative information. Items can only be corrected or removed if they are inaccurate, incomplete, misleading, unverifiable, or reporting past the statutory timeframe.

Credit monitoring continuously checks your credit files across Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Whenever a major change occurs—such as a new hard inquiry, a balance fluctuation, a public record update, or a bureau dispute response—you receive an alert so you can track progress and protect against identity theft.

To verify your identity with the bureaus, you will typically need a copy of a government-issued photo ID (driver's license or passport), proof of address (utility bill, lease, or bank statement dated within 60 days), and your Social Security number verification. For specific accounts, creditor statements or payment receipts are helpful.
